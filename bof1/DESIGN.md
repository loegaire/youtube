# Control Flow — Swiss Glass Direction

Control Flow is an editorial explanation, not a themed dashboard. Every frame begins with a strict twelve-column logic: chapter and title occupy a fixed masthead, the proof lives in the central field, and one conclusion closes the frame. Asymmetry, alignment, and negative space create the composition before color or effects are added.

Typography is the primary visual material. Helvetica Neue or Inter carries the narrative with compact Swiss hierarchy; IBM Plex Mono carries addresses, bytes, commands, and code. Titles are large and tightly tracked. Labels are small, uppercase, and widely tracked. Explanatory paragraphs are avoided when a diagram and one precise sentence can do the work.

The base is neutral charcoal, not green. Green appears as an undertone in selected glass planes and as the single recurring accent for valid data and forward motion. Amber identifies addresses, coral identifies corruption, and pale mint identifies CPU action. These semantic colors are used sparingly so a change in color remains meaningful.

Glassmorphism is treated as material, not spectacle. Panels use low-opacity white over the charcoal field, a one-pixel border, a faint top edge, and restrained depth. There are no luminous blobs, decorative particles, soft pills, or ornamental gradients. Corners stay nearly square and shadows are shallow.

Motion follows the same discipline. One causal action dominates at a time: a byte fills, a boundary is crossed, an address changes, or a pointer redirects. Elements enter with short ease-out motion and leave faster. Persistent atmospheric animation is forbidden; the still frame must already communicate the idea.
